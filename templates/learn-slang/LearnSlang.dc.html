chela

CHEH-lah · noun, feminine · informal

means a beer

Mexico Guatemala Honduras El Salvador
Gabriela L. · Native teacher, Guadalajara · Updated Jun 9, 2026
{{ t.label }}
The short version

Chela is how Mexicans say beer in casual settings. The plural is even more common: ¿Vamos por unas chelas?

Three ways you'll hear it

1
Inviting friends out

People order rounds, not single beers, so you'll mostly hear the plural.

¿Vamos por unas chelas?
Wanna grab some beers?
2
Ordering at a bar

Swap it for cerveza and you'll sound instantly more local.

Una chela bien fría, por favor.
A nice cold beer, please.
3
Buying for home

Works for store-bought beer too, not just on tap.

Voy a comprar unas chelas para el partido.
I'm gonna grab some beers for the game.
How casual is it?

Casual, but never rude

FormalNeutralCasualStreet

Fine with friends, coworkers and family in any relaxed setting. Use cerveza in writing for work.

What locals actually say · Gabriela

"Nobody orders una cerveza, por favor in a Mexican bar. What you'll hear every night is Échame una chela: literally, throw me a beer."

"Beer" around the Spanish world

Slang doesn't travel. Ask for a chela in Buenos Aires and you'll get blank looks.

Questions learners ask

Is chela rude?+

No. It's casual but respectable. Safe with anyone in an informal setting.

Chela or cheve?+

Same meaning. Chela is used everywhere in Mexico; cheve is more common in the north.

Can I use it in writing?+

In texts and social posts, yes. In work emails, stick with cerveza.
